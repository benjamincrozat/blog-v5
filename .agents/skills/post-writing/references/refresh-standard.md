# Existing Post Refresh Standard

Use this standard for a substantive revision of an existing post, especially when search demand, rankings, click-through rate, authority, or business value matters. Keep the working brief outside the article.

## Contents

- Capture the baseline
- Inspect the full page
- Inspect current search intent
- Use ranking evidence
- Choose the right action
- Plan the refresh
- Edit like an owner
- Measure the result

## Capture the Baseline

Record what is known before changing the page:

```text
URL and slug:
Research date:
Reader and dominant intent:
Search Console periods:
Important queries and metrics:
Current title and snippet behavior:
Incoming internal links and anchors:
Useful external links and mentions:
Winning material to preserve:
Weak, stale, or unsupported material:
Competing internal pages:
Existing screenshots inspected:
New evidence to create:
Business role:
Useful next action:
Search success metric:
Business success metric:
Known gaps:
```

- Use page-level Search Console data when access exists. Compare at least a recent 28-day and 90-day view when enough data is available.
- Record clicks, impressions, click-through rate, average position, device, country, and the main query groups that affect the decision.
- State when Search Console, analytics, conversion, or historical data is unavailable. Never invent volume, rankings, click-through rate, or conversion impact.
- Save the baseline outside the article so later reporting does not rely on memory.

## Inspect the Full Page

- Read the entire current article before proposing an outline. Identify accurate material worth preserving instead of assuming a rewrite is better.
- Open the rendered page when layout, code formatting, tables, embeds, or visual flow matters.
- Open every inline screenshot and important article image. Inspect the actual pixels at full size and at final article width.
- Check whether each image is readable, current, correctly cropped, safe, and tied to a nearby claim. Do not judge images from Markdown, alt text, filenames, dimensions, or counts.
- Verify the current title, description, headings, factual links, examples, version claims, and dates.
- Check whether another internal page answers the same intent better or competes for the same query.

## Inspect Current Search Intent

- Use `seo-growth-strategy` for ranking-sensitive work.
- Choose one to three representative queries from observed demand instead of reviewing every wording variation.
- Inspect live Google results with the built-in browser. Record the date and any location, language, or device assumption that may affect the result.
- Note the dominant intent, result types, AI Overview, featured snippet, People Also Ask, videos, forums, image pack, and other features that change what earns a click.
- Inspect enough relevant organic results to understand the pattern, usually the top three relevant pages. Stop when more pages repeat the same evidence.
- Compare their promise, freshness, proof, examples, visuals, and gaps. Do not use competitors as factual sources or copy their structure.
- Define what this article can add that existing results and a generic AI answer do not provide.

## Use Ranking Evidence

- Read [ranking-reality.md](ranking-reality.md). Treat Google's public advice as policy or product guidance, not a full account of ranking systems.
- Build the plan from Search Console, live results, court exhibits or internal-system evidence, controlled SEO tests, and this site's own experiments. Use forum tactics as hypotheses, not facts.
- Label each important SEO claim as confirmed mechanism, controlled test, site observation, plausible hypothesis, or folklore.
- Pick the smallest set of changes that tests a clear idea. Do not change title, intent, internal links, dates, and half the article at once when learning which change worked matters.
- Do not confuse a stored signal with a known ranking weight. Leaks can prove that a field or system exists without proving how strongly it affects this query.

## Choose the Right Action

Choose one action before drafting:

- **Leave alone:** the page still satisfies the intent and no meaningful improvement is supported.
- **Refresh:** the intent and URL remain right, but facts, proof, examples, visuals, or clarity can improve.
- **Merge:** another internal page serves the same intent and one stronger page should own it.
- **Narrow or split:** the page mixes distinct reader jobs that cannot be served well together.
- **Retire:** demand or usefulness no longer justifies keeping the page.

- Preserve the existing `id`, slug, and proven angle by default.
- Change a slug only when the current URL is seriously wrong and the redirect plan is clear.
- Do not change a winning title or opening only because a generic rule suggests a different format.

## Plan the Refresh

- Define the reader outcome, central promise, important claims, proof method, original contribution, limits, business role, and useful next action.
- Define the target query group, title-query match, first-screen promise, incoming internal-link targets, outgoing topic links, and the asset that could earn an external link or mention.
- List the parts to keep, update, remove, and add.
- For technical articles, build the fixture, reproduction, benchmark, matrix, or visual proof before rewriting around it.
- For a new library, framework release, or major feature, use the dedicated evidence rules for clean install, upgrade path, central feature test, support matrix, and adopt-or-wait decision.
- Plan screenshots only after inspecting every existing image. Keep, crop, replace, or remove each one for a stated reason.
- Prefer one strong page with real proof over extra sections added only for length or keyword coverage.

## Edit Like an Owner

- Keep verified material that still helps. Remove filler, repeated answers, stale version advice, copied documentation, and claims that cannot be supported.
- Give the answer early. Do not add generic `Introduction`, `Conclusion`, or FAQ sections.
- Add an FAQ only for distinct questions that deserve short standalone answers and are not already answered in the body.
- Choose a descriptive title without fixed character or word targets. Put the clearest form of the target query near the start when it fits the promise and keeps the visible search result useful. Generate alternatives only when they help solve a real framing problem.
- Make the main query and its close variants clear in the opening answer, useful headings, and body. Do not chase a fixed density or repeat the same phrase where a clearer word fits.
- Link directly to current primary evidence. Add contextual incoming links from relevant strong pages and useful outgoing links within the topic cluster. Use descriptive anchors and do not rely only on a related-posts block.
- When freshness matters, use a real visible date or year and keep it consistent with front matter, structured data, and the sitemap. Change `modified_at` only after a meaningful update is ready to publish.
- Make the title and snippet earn the right click, then satisfy the same promise immediately on the page. Do not use curiosity that attracts the wrong searcher.
- Keep a service or contact path restrained and relevant to the reader's problem.

## Measure the Result

- Record the publication date, material changes, target query groups, and final screenshots or artifacts.
- Record the SEO hypothesis, evidence level, internal-link changes, and any distribution or link-earning action.
- Use `file-first-posts` to verify the rendered page, images, front matter, sync, and publishing state.
- Do not submit a sitemap, request indexing, or change Search Console without explicit approval.
- Review leading search indicators after two clean 28-day periods when enough data exists. Use a longer window for low-volume pages.
- Compare query groups and intent, not only the total URL traffic.
- Track business outcomes separately when possible: contact clicks, qualified inquiries, package interest, newsletter signups, or another named action.
- Report uncertainty. A ranking or conversion change after publication does not prove that one edit caused it.
